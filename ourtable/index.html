OurTable

A family kitchen on the web. One shared recipe book where every recipe answers one question first: can everyone at the table eat this? Dietary checks, recipes that remember how you actually cook, and safety rules you can test.

The Problem

Cooking for a household is a coordination problem

This app started because of my wife. She's a passionate home cook who saves recipes from TikTok and Instagram — and then can't find them when she needs them. Screenshots buried in camera roll, links in DMs that disappear, recipes saved to five different apps with no connection between them.

But the bigger problem isn't recipe management — it's that cooking for a household involves multiple people with different dietary needs, different schedules, and a shared fridge nobody has a full picture of. No app solves this as a household coordination problem. They all solve it as an individual food tracker.

🤌

Recipes live everywhere

TikTok, Instagram, screenshots, links in messages — no unified home for saved recipes that actually stays organized.

🧑‍🍳

Cooking for more than one person

No app thinks about the household as the unit. Who's eating tonight? What does the fridge actually have? What can we make?

What It Does

Six things that solve the household problem

🍽️

The table

The home screen is a table with a seat for everyone. Each seat shows whether that person can eat tonight’s dish, and tapping it says what to swap. Tap a name to take someone off tonight’s table and the recipe scales to who is eating.

✍️

Our version

Say what you changed in plain English. OurTable turns it into structured changes beside the original, which is never overwritten. The dietary check runs on your version.

🥘

What can I make?

Type what you have. It searches only your own saved recipes, checks everyone’s diets, lists what is missing and can add it to the grocery list.

🛟

Recipe Rescue

Dinner went wrong? Four possible answers, with safety limits that are fixed rules: food left out too long is always “don’t eat this”, and undercooked meat always needs a thermometer.

📅

Plan, fridge and grocery

A weekly dinner planner, a fridge list that flags what expires first, and a shared grocery list that fills from any recipe.

🏆

Trophy cabinet

Twelve illustrated trophies in four tracks, plus weekly stamps, that reward logging your changes. “Family Secret”: change a recipe three times and cook it three times, and it’s yours.

Design Decisions

Why it works the way it does

→
AI is for language. Code is for safety.

Understanding a sentence is fuzzy work a model could help with later. Diets, allergens, quantities and food safety are not. They come from fixed rules with automated tests, and a model is never allowed to assert them. The diet check is a keyword check on ingredient names, so it is a guide, not a guarantee. No AI model runs in the app today.

→
Household as the unit, not the individual

The first question in sign-up is “who’s at your table?”. People who don’t need an account, like kids and grandparents, still get a seat with their own dietary needs. The tradeoff is a slightly longer sign-up. The payoff is that warnings work from the first recipe.

→
Never overwrite the original

Changes live beside the recipe, each one switchable. “Our version” is computed from the original plus the active changes, so you can always get back to the recipe as written. Unreadable input is kept as a note, never dropped.

→
Three looks, Midnight by default

Feedback on the first web build was that it felt too homey for everyone, so I built three looks: Hearth (warm), Midnight (dark) and Market (bright). Midnight is the default. The cost is three themes to keep readable, and I have not yet tested which one people prefer.

→
Reward the behaviour that makes the product work

Recipe evolution only works if people log their changes. The trophies are tied to that habit, with progress shown left to right along a path, so you can always see what is next.

→
Demo first, accounts second

Anyone can try the app in seconds on sample data with no account, and nothing leaves their browser. The real Supabase backend was tested separately with throwaway accounts. The cost is that the demo cannot show two people sharing a kitchen.

Build Status

Active build · Working web app

Built and working in the demo: the household table with per-person dietary checks, recipe book, recipe import from pasted text (link import works in the connected version, not in the demo), weekly planner, fridge and grocery lists, cook mode with step timers, recipe evolution, recipe profiles and similar recipes, the Ask page, Recipe Rescue, the trophy cabinet and three looks.

Designed earlier, not in the web build: the AI fridge scanner, Party Mode, cost per serving, food-waste tracking and pricing tiers, from a 45-screen native-app concept.

Not done yet: testing with real households, syncing cook history and photos across devices, a legal review, any AI model layer, and a wider allergen list (egg, shellfish, soy and sesame are not checked yet).
